## 1. Executive result

H5 separated six topologies that had previously been overloaded under one word: semantic relation, active propagation, dependency/invalidation, selection/workspace, physical placement and authority.

Fresh ClayGraph G2a exposes a seventh topology that H5 only implied:

> **Which outcome is allowed to update which memory, relation, path, route or Assembly?**

H6 names this **causal-credit topology**.

The new compact laws are:

> `what influenced outcome != what receives learning credit != what may become live != what may act`

> `recoverable topology != harmless topology`

> `atomic switch != cognitive correctness`

> `effect expressibility != effect authority`

The last law is sharpened by concurrent ClayCode work: a genuinely general-purpose language may keep powerful/admin/destructive operations expressible as typed intents. Safety comes from non-forgeable host authority, effect-time validation and bounded live activation, not from pretending the operations cannot be represented at all.

The H6 architecture therefore separates fast per-query adaptation from slower learning and still slower live topology promotion:

`query -> context-active topology -> outcome -> scoped causal-credit receipt -> candidate policy/topology version -> validation/canary -> host permit -> atomic live-ref switch -> health shadow -> rollback if needed`.

## 3. Fusion 1 — why H5 is incomplete

H5 could answer:
- what relation exists;
- what currently propagates;
- what invalidates;
- what is selected/broadcast;
- where it physically executes;
- what may act.

It could not answer:

> **After an outcome, which upstream structures may learn from it?**

This is not identical to propagation. An item may have propagated but not caused the result. It is not identical to selection. A selected item may be harmless while a downstream projection causes the failure. It is not identical to authority. A reward cannot mint capability. It is not identical to truth. Utility cannot rewrite canonical facts.

H6 therefore adds:

### T7 — CAUSAL_CREDIT

Candidate scopes:

`ITEM | PATH | ASSEMBLY | ROUTE | UNKNOWN`

Each credit carries:
- causal-stage receipts;
- scope;
- confidence;
- verifier/estimator identity;
- control-arm identity;
- source graph/activation/query/selection/workspace/outcome references;
- `no_authority = true`.

Default rule:

> **UNKNOWN positive credit is bounded and non-broadcasting until further evidence resolves it.**

## 8. CARTS architecture

H6 retains H5's six topologies and adds T7:

1. **Semantic relation topology** — exact roles, identity, provenance, time, collection semantics.
2. **Active propagation topology** — gain/depth/decay/inhibition/budget/diversity.
3. **Dependency/invalidation topology** — `KNOWN_VALID | KNOWN_INVALID | UNKNOWN`.
4. **Selection/workspace topology** — query/candidate/system-workspace/projection/internal-uptake identity.
5. **Physical topology** — U2 PlacementVector axes.
6. **Authority topology** — externally granted capability/effect path.
7. **Causal-credit topology** — what may learn from which outcome and with what confidence.

### Live topology objects

`TopologyCandidate`
- model/compiler constructible;
- versioned;
- non-live;
- has rollback parent.

`TopologyIntent`
- serializable;
- can express powerful changes;
- non-authoritative.

`TopologyPermit`
- opaque host capability;
- not constructible from model text;
- binds current live hash, candidate hash, scope, impact budget, basis/selection/revocation/closure where relevant, canary/health policy and rollback target.

`LiveTopologyRef`
- effect-relevant current active topology;
- old-or-new atomicity target;
- post-switch monitoring and rollback remain required for consequential changes.

This preserves general-purpose expressibility while denying ambient authority.

## 9. The two-timescale correction

H6 makes an important temporal distinction:

### Fast adaptation
Per-query:
- route choice;
- active Assembly;
- propagation gain/depth;
- contextual bypass/supplement/escalation.

### Slow adaptation
Across outcomes:
- learned salience;
- routing policy parameters;
- topology candidate generation;
- only scoped credit updates.

### Slower live promotion
For consequential learned topology:
- shadow/candidate construction;
- semantic/admissibility validation;
- canary evaluation;
- host permit;
- atomic repoint;
- health/causal shadow;
- rollback.

The hypothesis is that **fast cognition does not require fast unvalidated self-modification of the live topology**.
