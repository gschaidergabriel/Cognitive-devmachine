# ATOMIC — DCA-001 Distinguishing Context Assembly

## Final verdict

**DCA-001 is refuted as a distinct context-selection/runtime architecture.** A strong conventional safe decision-focused greedy cover matches DCA's decision-certification behavior in the local synthetic discriminator, while DCA pays additional witness overhead.

A narrower candidate survives: **make completeness/currentness a typed, non-collapsible boundary of every model-visible bounded CognitiveView** so Frank cannot silently interpret omitted, incomplete or stale evidence as certified absence.

## Why this problem was selected

The fresh swarm already occupies effect-generation fencing, V17 causal closure, CARF causal credit, CCW terminal closure, CQC causal quotienting and UnifiedDB schema/recovery coupling. Frank v0.1 nevertheless still has an open whole-system obligation: authoritative state must become a model-visible bounded assembly, and the global source explicitly forbids whole-repository context dumps when a bounded assembly suffices.

G9 already sketches `materialize(Query) -> CognitiveView`, `ViewDelta` with `dependency_hash` and `selector_hash`, and `current_answer_context = AnswerCapsule + relevant(ViewDelta)`. What it does not yet make explicit is the epistemic status of information missing from that bounded payload.

## Core distinction

A fact missing from the model-visible context can mean at least four different things:

1. it is present in the assembly;
2. it is absent and the relevant query scope is certified complete;
3. it was not loaded or the source scope is incomplete/unknown;
4. an earlier completeness certificate existed but is stale after a material delta.

DCA proposes that these states must not collapse at the model boundary.

## Hypothesis search

Fourteen hypotheses covered full-view deletion, relevance top-k, diversity/submodular selection, diagnostic test cover, information bottleneck compression, query-relative completeness, negative-space witnesses, epoch binding, incremental dependency invalidation, Hyperposition branch-separating bases, a dual-channel typed membrane, predictive-suppression gating, fault-diagnosis transfer and an explicit no-new-runtime conventional baseline.

## External prior-art destruction

The broad novelty claim fails. Information Bottleneck already formalizes short representations preserving relevant information. Interactive/submodular set cover and recent hypothesis-testing work cover minimum-cost information selection. Database work formalizes query-relative completeness in partially complete databases. Why/why-not provenance covers missing-answer explanations. A 2026 LLM study directly reports unstable negative closure under partial evidence and studies coverage-certificate elicitation.

Therefore DCA does not claim new compression mathematics, new set-cover mathematics, new completeness theory or discovery of LLM over-closure.

## Local decision-assembly discriminator

A deterministic seed-20260811 synthetic benchmark generated 3,000 finite-hypothesis/action cases with token-costed information items and mandatory identity/intent witnesses.

- FULL: certification 1.0000; mean cost ~165.99.
- TOPK_SAFE: certification 0.9153; mean cost ~27.84; mean uncovered material pairs ~0.3013.
- SAFE_DECISION_COVER: certification 0.9743; mean cost ~13.35; mean uncovered pairs ~0.0853.
- DCA_SELECTION: certification 0.9743; mean cost ~15.35; same uncovered pairs ~0.0853.

This is the selector-architecture refutation. DCA does not beat the strong conventional decision-cover selector; the witness makes the proxy slightly more expensive.

## Negative/completeness discriminator

A second deterministic benchmark generated 8,000 partial-completeness/negative-query cases, including stale-certificate attacks.

- TOPK_NAIVE: over-closure 31.60%; selective accuracy 68.40%.
- QUERY_AWARE_NO_CERT: over-closure 14.94%; selective accuracy 85.06%.
- GLOBAL_CERT: over-closure 3.44%; selective accuracy 94.74%; UNKNOWN 34.69%.
- QR_STALE_UNFENCED: over-closure 9.99%; stale over-closure 9.99%.
- DCA_QR_CURRENT: over-closure 0%; stale over-closure 0%; selective accuracy 100%; UNKNOWN 29.10%.

The DCA result is not magical accuracy. It is conservative semantics: unsupported negative answers are converted into UNKNOWN/EXPAND. The strong result is zero encoded over-closure under the benchmark assumptions, not increased answer coverage.

## Currentness matters

Query-relative completeness without epoch/dependency binding fails the stale-state control. A prior certificate can say a query scope was complete, then a new relevant fact arrives, and a stale context can incorrectly claim absence. DCA therefore binds negative closure to current semantic/state epoch plus selector/dependency/projection identity.

This should reuse current G9 `dependency_hash` and `selector_hash`; a second invalidation subsystem is not justified.

## ClayCode candidate

The Clay artifact defines a `DistinguishingContextAssembly` containing:

- hard witnesses;
- selected evidence payload;
- `DistinctionWitness`;
- query-relative `QueryCompletenessWitness`;
- `CurrentnessWitness`;
- explicit READY / EXPAND / ABSTAIN / REFRESH states.

Legal missing-material states include PRESENT, CERTIFIED_ABSENT, NOT_LOADED_OR_INCOMPLETE_UNKNOWN and STALE_CERTIFICATE. UNKNOWN and STALE may never lower to false/absent.

The assembly carries zero truth, SELF, user-intent or effect authority.

## Relation to Hyperposition/GWT/Needle

Hyperposition: a branch omitted from context is not refuted; unresolved material alternatives must remain explicit when the budget cannot distinguish them.

GWT: salience/broadcast cannot certify database completeness.

Needle: a missing tool/capability/blocker fact in the current bounded view cannot be interpreted as nonexistence unless the relevant capability query is certified complete and current.

Memory/reward/goals: the model must not convert forgotten/not-loaded evidence into negative world-state updates merely because the context window omitted it.

## Strongest counterhypothesis

The conventional baseline currently wins at runtime/selector level:

`ordinary query-aware CognitiveView + safe decision-focused cover + query-relative completeness metadata + existing selector/dependency hashes`.

DCA survives only as a language/interface experiment: does one typed non-collapsible boundary reject unsafe compiler/lowering/translator/runtime mutants that an ordinary metadata bundle allows?

## Kill criteria

Kill DCA completely if the conventional metadata bundle matches:

- omission-versus-absence safety;
- stale-certificate rejection;
- protected decision distinction preservation;
- hard identity/intent/authority field retention;
- mutation rejection before execution;
- false-positive rate on valid programs;
- token/CPU/RSS/storage/replay cost;
- context reuse under irrelevant deltas;

with lower implementation complexity.
