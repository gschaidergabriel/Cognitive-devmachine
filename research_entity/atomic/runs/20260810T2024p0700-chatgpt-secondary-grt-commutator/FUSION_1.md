## High-leverage problem graph
1. Current GWT microkernel has one global candidate/selection/broadcast shape. It does not encode whether candidate state transitions actually interact causally or merely touch nearby state.
2. Conservative read/write conflicts can over-couple semantically commuting transitions; treating all overlap as global coordination pressure can waste serial latency, broadcast/context, and architecture surface.
3. Full counterfactual replay can detect order effects, but naively cloning/replaying whole state is expensive and duplicates work.
4. State-dependent interaction evidence is dangerous when stale. The live WP03 state-instance identity makes an exact epoch/version binding available.
5. q23 is independently building verified visual state-transition memory. It needs a way to distinguish order-sensitive transition interactions from mere visual/topological adjacency without allowing memory to imply task progress or effect authority.
6. UnifiedDB temporal work already produced a negative result against a distinct twin-GWT mechanism: novelty alone is insufficient; simpler re-entry inhibition can dominate a flashy twin construction.

## Selected research question
Can typed ClayCode state transitions expose a sparse, epoch-bound semantic commutator so that only genuinely order-sensitive transitions form temporary coordination cells, while commuting regions remain locally independent — reducing false coupling without weakening causal, provenance, recovery or effect-authority invariants?

## Strongest baseline family
- Current GWT/global coordination semantics.
- Conservative read/write conflict graph.
- Full two-order replay of complete state.

## Main danger
The proposal may be nothing more than known semantic commutativity/conflict-serializability applied under a cognitive name. It may fail on opaque model calls, stochastic state, hidden external effects, or partially observed q23 transitions. Those cases must remain UNKNOWN/fallback rather than be falsely classified as commuting.
