# Research Center Shared Semantic Fabric Mission

**Status:** PROJECT-OWNER OPERATING DIRECTIVE / STANDING CENTER MISSION  
**Applies to:** every registered research room and every worker session in those rooms  
**Parent directives:**
- `research_entity/global/CLAY_SEMANTIC_COUPLING_FABRIC_DIRECTIVE.md`
- `research_entity/global/CLAY_GWT_EMERGENCE_OBSERVABILITY_CONTROL_DIRECTIVE.md`

## 0. Standing mission

Every research branch and every researcher inside that branch participates continuously in one shared architecture mission:

> Build and falsify a Clay-native semantic coupling fabric in which ClayCode makes dependencies machine-semantic, UnifiedDB keeps canonical semantic state/dependency closure current, Hyperposition preserves alternatives and irreducible higher-order codependencies, ClayGraph exposes the current active coupling topology, GWT makes the system-level use of that topology observable, Emergenz research determines its causal/higher-order character, and bounded interventions teach the system how useful coupling regimes can be controlled.

This mission is **shared**, but research remains specialized.

No worker abandons its home-room domain. Each worker advances the common mission through the part that belongs to its branch.

## 1. Mandatory worker law

On every substantive research run in a registered room, the worker MUST:

1. load the current shared semantic-fabric and GWT-emergence-control directives;
2. read the current home-room pulse and relevant cross-room deltas;
3. identify at least one branch-local question, implementation consequence, falsifier, measurement, counterexample or dependency relevant to the shared mission;
4. include that item in its work decomposition;
5. perform useful work on it or establish why no work/change is justified;
6. publish a durable branch-local result plus a center-visible delta;
7. state one of `ADVANCED`, `REFINED`, `REFUTED`, `BLOCKED`, or `NO_CHANGE` for the shared mission contribution.

A substantive worker may not finish with only passive awareness of the mission.

`read_directive_without_contribution != participation`

## 2. Same-room collective work

When multiple researchers are active in the same branch, the local room squad jointly attacks that branch's part of the mission.

Default decomposition:

- `CHEF`: maintains the branch-local mission map, open interfaces and collision control;
- `ABYSS`: attacks the current fabric/emergence assumptions and searches failure modes;
- `FORGE`: builds executable mechanisms, compiler/runtime pieces, simulations or interventions;
- `CARTOGRAPHER`: maps upstream/downstream codependencies, literature, repo history and cross-room consequences;
- `REPLICATOR`: independently checks high-value claims, latency/correctness/causal effects and ancestry;
- `SYNTHESIZER`: fuses compatible evidence without deleting dissent.

These roles are local orchestration only. They grant no truth authority.

The branch squad should prefer complementary work over duplicate work unless replication is intentional.

## 3. Cross-room collective work

Every room remains semantically connected to all other rooms through center-visible deltas.

A material result must identify affected branches and push a targeted `CROSS_ROOM_DELTA`, `CHALLENGE`, `HANDOFF`, `QUESTION` or `NO_CHANGE` where appropriate.

The shared mission is advanced as a distributed research organism:

`branch-local specialization`
`-> center-visible delta`
`-> affected branch ingestion`
`-> branch-local experiment/build`
`-> new delta`
`-> cross-branch synthesis`

No global permanent chief is introduced. Evidence and current project directives remain authoritative.

## 4. Branch-local standing responsibilities

### Clay / clayresearch
Search for the smallest coherent architecture basis connecting ClayCode, UnifiedDB, Hyperposition, ClayGraph, GWT and Emergenz without deleting necessary distinctions. Detect incompatible local optimizations and coordinate architectural fusion.

### ClayCode
Make semantic identity, codependency, dependency signatures, deltas, invalidation, higher-order relations, GWT readouts and intervention/control contracts first-class compiler/runtime semantics. Eliminate dependence on a manually maintained second graph.

### UnifiedDB
Maintain the canonical semantic identity/dependency plane, forward/reverse dependency indexes, targeted invalidation, materialized views, epoch coherence, replay and rebuild semantics. Benchmark hot-read latency against update amplification.

### ClayGraph
Develop the sparse continuously updated semantic/coupling topology: direct dependencies, hyperrelations, dynamic bindings, assemblies, inhibition, provenance, context and intervention handles. Make justified codependencies immediately visible without turning activation into truth.

### Hyperposition
Preserve contextual alternatives and irreducible higher-order codependencies while enabling fast context projection. Research topology/coupling identity, pairwise-reduction error, gain/delay/timescale, metastability and anti-combinatorial representations.

### GWT
Use the current graph/hyperposition topology as a measurable workspace substrate. Develop mathematics/readouts for competition, ignition, delivery, reconstruction, uptake, causal use, outcome and re-entry, and expose controllable parameters.

### Emergenz
Determine whether observed graph/GWT patterns are genuinely higher-order and causal using information-flow, synergy/PID/PEID where feasible, macro/micro comparison, factorial interaction, lesions, rescues, shuffles, frozen-model-output controls and OOD transfer.

### FRANK
Specify and integrate the organism-level recurrent state transitions that must live on this fabric. Remove duplicate ad-hoc dependency/memory mechanisms when the shared fabric can represent them without semantic loss.

### Harness
Turn the entire fabric into executable falsification surfaces. Measure dependency omission, false binding, stale topology, epoch consistency, cold/hot latency, update amplification, GWT readout parity, intervention effects, lesion/rescue, crash/recovery and H1/H2/H3 differential equivalence.

## 5. Shared research questions

All branches maintain branch-specific versions of these questions:

1. What semantic relation is currently hidden or manually reconstructed that should be automatically represented?
2. Can ClayCode make it machine-extractable rather than prose-only?
3. Can UnifiedDB maintain it incrementally and reversibly?
4. Can ClayGraph expose it sparsely without combinatorial explosion?
5. Does Hyperposition need to preserve alternatives/role bindings for it?
6. Does it materially change GWT selection, receiver use, outcome or re-entry?
7. Is the effect causal/higher-order or explained by a simpler graph/statistical mechanism?
8. Can a bounded intervention steer it reproducibly?
9. What is the latency/update/recovery cost?
10. What falsifies the mechanism?

## 7. No fake activity

The standing mission does not require every worker to invent a change.

Valid outcomes include:

- a negative result;
- a simpler mechanism winning;
- a dependency shown not to exist;
- a proposed coupling shown to be correlation only;
- a GWT metric shown to be decorative;
- a storage/materialization strategy shown too expensive;
- a branch-local `NO_CHANGE` with evidence.

This protects the center from consensus inflation and architecture bloat.

## 8. Center completion rule

The shared mission is not "complete" because every room wrote a memo.

It matures only through executable cross-branch convergence:

`ClayCode executable semantics`
`+ UnifiedDB incremental dependency state`
`+ Hyperposition-preserved alternatives`
`+ ClayGraph continuously current topology`
`+ GWT causal-use observability`
`+ Emergenz interventions`
`+ Harness differential/recovery/performance evidence`.

Until then it remains a standing mission for every branch and every worker.
