## Open hypotheses worth keeping

### H1 — rich external substrate improves reflective agency

**Hypothesis:** useful reflective agency becomes more reliable when state, memory, perception, arbitration, effect receipts and verified outcome re-entry exist outside the language model.

**Engineering confidence:** 0.82 that this is a productive architecture hypothesis; this is not a scientific probability.

**Falsifier:** a controlled matched system where removing those external substrate functions does not reduce task completion, recovery, causal attribution or safe termination across preregistered workloads.

### H2 — Global J-Space can become a useful causal coordination interface

**Hypothesis:** model-specific internal/vector control can be bound to typed workspace state strongly enough to improve task-relevant behavior without collapsing into prompt, label, codebook or nuisance shortcuts.

**Engineering confidence:** 0.55.

**Falsifier:** preregistered label-free, counterbalanced, family-disjoint interventions with matched nuisance controls repeatedly fail to produce selective behavioral effects, or nuisance interventions explain equal/greater variance.

### H3 — UnifiedDB + typed projections reduce state-fragmentation failure

**Hypothesis:** one canonical durable substrate plus rebuildable projections reduces ownership ambiguity, stale cross-service state and recovery complexity relative to the historical multi-store design.

**Engineering confidence:** 0.85.

**Falsifier:** matched failure/recovery tests show no reduction in inconsistent ownership, replay ambiguity or recovery defects, or show that the unified substrate creates a worse single-point bottleneck without compensating controls.

## Negative results are part of the architecture

The project should retain evidence of rumination/fixation, prompt-vs-weight conflict, context contamination, latency/timeouts, integration failures, dead modules, provider/control violations, zero-yield data conversion, and failed causal controls. These are not embarrassing footnotes: they explain why later architecture moved toward explicit state, typed causal identity, deterministic gates, provenance and fail-closed promotion.
