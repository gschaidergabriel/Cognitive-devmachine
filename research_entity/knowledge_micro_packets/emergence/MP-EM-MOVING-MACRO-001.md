### Core

The most predictive or intervention-useful causal description may change within one task: microstate, local assembly, WorkspaceEpisode, recurring organ-candidate and back. A fixed claim that one macro-level always wins may itself be a Goodhart error.

### Evidence / boundary

Causal-emergence formalisms make macro-vs-micro comparison possible, but results depend on coarse-graining, intervention distributions and model class. The moving-level proposal is speculative and remains GWT-consistent only because every candidate macrostate is grounded in bounded selection, availability, receiver use and re-entry.

### Implication

UnifiedDB and ClayGraph should preserve multiple synchronized resolutions rather than prematurely canonizing one level. A macrostate is useful only if it improves matched predictive or control performance.

### Next test

Across identical trajectories, run sliding-window comparisons among preregistered micro, assembly, WorkspaceEpisode, organ and random/task-label coarse-grainings under matched information and model complexity.
