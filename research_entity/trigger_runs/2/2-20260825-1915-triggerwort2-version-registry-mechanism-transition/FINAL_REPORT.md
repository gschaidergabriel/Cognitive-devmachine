## 7. External research delta — mechanism transition matters

Current primary research was rechecked during this Trigger-2 cycle.

### Variable binding training dynamics

Wu, Geiger & Milliere, *How Do Transformers Learn Variable Binding in Symbolic Programs?*, ICML 2025 / arXiv:2505.20896, reports three observed phases in their trained Transformer:

1. random prediction;
2. shallow heuristic prioritizing early assignments;
3. systematic variable-dereferencing mechanism.

Their causal interventions support the later mechanism interpretation.

This does not establish how Qwen3.5-0.8B will train. It establishes a serious counterhypothesis to an endpoint-only Trigger-2 evaluation: a final high score can hide a training regime that relied on shallow shortcuts before the desired mechanism emerged, or can leave residual shortcuts undetected.

### Steering reliability

Braun et al., arXiv:2505.22637, report high sample/prompt variance in steering effects and better steering where activation-difference directions are more coherent and class activations more separable.

Implication: one global J-Space vector result must not be generalized across prompt families or checkpoints. Coherence/separability and matched controls belong in the model-specific gate.

### Causal mediation vs probes

Sankaranarayanan et al., arXiv:2602.16080, report that Generative Causal Mediation selected components that outperformed correlational probe baselines for steering on their tested tasks/models.

Implication: linear decodability remains useful measurement but cannot by itself promote a semantic/J-Space control claim. Where internals are accessible, Trigger-2 should prefer preregistered intervention evidence with controls.

### Continual-SFT retention candidate

Chen et al., arXiv:2605.29495, report reduced forgetting with on-policy replay on tested 7-8B continual-SFT backbones.

Implication: on-policy replay is a later matched-budget ablation candidate, not a default for Qwen3.5-0.8B. Scale/base/task mismatch forbids direct transfer of the result.

### Binding checkpoint rule

Before any pilot, freeze a normalized checkpoint schedule. The contract currently offers the preregistration candidates:

`0.00, 0.01, 0.03, 0.10, 0.25, 0.50, 0.75, 1.00`

These are candidate normalized optimizer-progress points, not a claim that all must be used. The final schedule must be fixed before training/holdout inspection.

At each retained binding checkpoint, evaluate both the intended task and shortcut baselines:

- record/focus position heuristic;
- template/lexical nuisance;
- codebook display-order / target-slot / symbol-frequency nuisance;
- exact model-visible nuisance signature after semantic ablation;
- a simple learned nuisance-only challenger evaluated across disjoint nuisance families.

A final high task score is not sufficient while a nuisance/heuristic predictor remains above preregistered tolerance.

### Hyperposition dynamics

The Hyperposition carrier remains present from the first episode. Checkpoint evaluation also tracks:

- branch-set retrieval;
- branch permutation invariance;
- branch-local evidence/counterevidence/falsifier/validity/provenance/status attachment;
- preservation under non-discriminating updates;
- localized change under a discriminating observation;
- projection != deletion;
- same-ancestry repetition != independent evidence;
- discriminator adequacy when unresolved.

### J-Space promotion ladder

The new contract distinguishes:

- `J0_OBSERVED_ACTIVATION`
