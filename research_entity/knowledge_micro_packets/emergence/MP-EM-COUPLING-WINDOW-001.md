### Core

A bounded Global Workspace may have an interior operating region: undercoupling prevents reconstruction and joint use, while overcoupling globalizes errors, erodes specialization, increases latency and allows winner monopolization. Functional emergence should therefore peak in a robust region rather than at maximum communication or graph density.

### Evidence / boundary

Shared-workspace architectures support coordination, while multi-agent evidence shows that added coordination can harm some task structures. This is not yet direct evidence for Frank or for a phase transition.

### Implication

Workspace capacity, gain, delay and re-entry strength must be experimental variables. Optimization must include transfer, specialization, perseveration, instability, latency, compute and memory shadows.

### Next test

Construct a coupling phase diagram in a deterministic RCWE microworld, then repeat across tasks, seeds and two implementations. The hypothesis fails if performance, transfer and stability rise monotonically with coupling under matched costs.
