## 1. Why this run changed the training priority

The newest Claude/Xeno vector-language evidence narrows the model deficit. At the exact measured Qwen3.5-0.8B snapshot, semantic reading is already strong on the measured lexically separated family, symbol-axis writeout is causally steerable, and whitened carrier-space HRR/MAP binding is measured at 1.0 through k<=16. The missing capability is therefore not best described as "learn semantics" or "learn how to emit symbols". The best current target is **content-dependent episodic routing/binding**:

`decoded semantic state -> episode-local mapping/codebook -> correct output symbol`

This run therefore creates candidate data for that missing computation while protecting the already-present read/write/carrier functions.

## 6. New training hypothesis produced by this run

### H_BINDING_ROUTER_AS_TARGETED_INSTALL

**Claim:** because semantic readout, output-axis writeability and carrier-space binding are already present at measured scope, a targeted curriculum emphasizing episode-local remapping plus context/template invariance should require less destructive model change than broad semantic retraining.

**Counterhypothesis:** the observed read/write components are not compositionally connectable by a small learned router; successful binding may require broad distributed changes that also alter semantic representations.

**Discriminator:** compare a narrow binding-focused full-FT curriculum against a broader GRF/HCU curriculum at matched train tokens/optimizer budget, then require both held-out remapping gains and <=2% retention damage on protected read/write/protocol anchors. This is a future training/evaluation design, not a performed experiment.

## 9. Research return into Clay

The Claude measurements imply an architecture/training distinction that should persist in Clay research:

- **representation availability** and **runtime variable binding** are separate capacities;
- a linearly readable concept does not imply prompt-local symbol binding;
- a writable output direction does not imply the network will dynamically route semantic state into that direction;
- high VSA/HRR algebra capacity in a normalized carrier space does not establish the existence of the learned routing circuit that decides what to bind;
- therefore future J-Space experiments should measure `READ`, `BIND/ROUTE`, and `WRITE` as distinct causal stages, not one monolithic "semantic vector" capability.

This is the strongest architecture implication of the current evidence and should inform both the future `go` harness and post-FT lesion/rescue design.
