## 3. External research

### 3.1 Adaptive recurrence / exit policy

Primary paper: Popescu, Borde & Liò, *Adaptive Depth in Looped Transformers: Diagnosing Learned Halting Gates and Trajectory Readouts*, arXiv:2607.20519 (2026).

Material result used here: learned halting behavior must be decomposed into trajectory formation and exit/readout quality. On the studied synthetic tasks and Ouro checkpoints, simple post-hoc confidence readouts on frozen trajectories often matched or outperformed learned linear/MLP gates. This is not direct evidence that the same policy wins in FRANK, but it creates a strong baseline requirement: a learned FRANK recurrence gate is not justified until it beats simple frozen-trajectory readouts under matched cost and quality.

Historical primary baselines reinforce the broader adaptive-compute hypothesis but do not determine FRANK's implementation. Graves' Adaptive Computation Time (arXiv:1603.08983) demonstrates learned variable recurrent compute; Universal Transformers (arXiv:1807.03819) combine recurrent depth with adaptive halting. Recent recurrent-depth work (arXiv:2604.07822) reports improved depth extrapolation with more inference recurrence but also an overthinking regime where excessive recurrence degrades predictions. Recent depth-conditioned looped-transformer ASR work (arXiv:2606.04678) independently demonstrates that recurrent depth can be a usable test-time compute axis outside autoregressive reasoning.

Inference for FRANK: recurrence depth should be treated as a controlled resource with explicit baselines and overthinking/false-halt measurements, not as an intrinsic virtue and not as proof of emergence.

### 3.2 Small local model + verifier

Primary paper: Zhang et al., *Small Language Models Need Strong Verifiers to Self-Correct Reasoning*, arXiv:2404.17140.

The paper reports that <=13B models can improve self-correction, but weak self-verifiers are a limitation and stronger GPT-4-based verification materially helps in their experiments. This does not imply FRANK must always call a strong peer. It supports retaining `strong peer verifier available` as an independent experimental arm for difficult/high-risk corrections rather than baking trust in the 4B's own confidence into the recurrence controller.

GNW literature describes nonlinear ignition, recurrent amplification and global availability/broadcast to specialized processors, but recent analysis also emphasizes that generic large-scale integration is not uniquely diagnostic of broadcasting. FRANK therefore keeps its H1 stage-separated receiver-causal receipts and event-bus/null comparisons. A high integration score, broad addressability, or recurrence alone cannot be promoted as GWT-like causal broadcast or consciousness.

## 4. Fusion 1: live hypotheses and counterhypotheses

The strongest recurrence hypothesis is no longer 'learn a smarter gate'. It is a three-way discrimination problem:

`trajectory formation quality x simple readout quality x learned gate quality`.

A single-state controller is cheap and responsive but can react to transient low-delta observations. A short frozen-trajectory post-hoc readout can reject some transient false halts but may delay legitimate exits. A learned gate may improve the frontier, but it may also shape trajectories badly or overfit training distributions. Fixed-depth remains an important null because adaptive control can lose once controller overhead and calibration failures are counted.

The strongest cross-domain counterpressure is ActiveAssembly quality. If the 4B receives a conflict-starved or evidence-starved active slice, residual pressure and apparent non-convergence may be caused by selection, not by the recurrence controller. Recurrence optimization therefore needs a factorial Assembly-policy x recurrence-policy experiment before promotion.

## 6. Fusion 2 / adversarial re-analysis

The build does NOT establish that the new readout is better than the existing controller. The nine tests establish deterministic contract behavior, not comparative task performance.

Potential failure modes preserved:

1. **latency tax**: requiring a minimum trajectory may overshoot easy solutions that a calibrated single-state controller could halt immediately;
2. **median masking**: a short median window can hide abrupt new evidence or urgency;
3. **threshold brittleness**: hand-set thresholds may transfer poorly across loop families/timescales;
4. **coupled-cause error**: high pressure may come from bad ActiveAssembly selection rather than need for more recurrence;
5. **OOD dynamics**: target-4B trajectories may show regimes absent from H1 synthetic/reference traces;
6. **readout/trajectory feedback**: once a readout actually controls continuation, it changes the future trajectory and ceases to be purely post-hoc in deployment;
7. **cost blindness**: compute cost and wall-clock latency are not yet measured in the new baseline;
8. **verifier dependence**: confidence/utility signals may be poorly calibrated without independent outcome verification;
9. **authority confusion risk**: recurrence/diagnostic receipts must remain non-authoritative even when they trigger routing toward effect-capable subsystems.

Therefore architecture promotion is intentionally withheld.
