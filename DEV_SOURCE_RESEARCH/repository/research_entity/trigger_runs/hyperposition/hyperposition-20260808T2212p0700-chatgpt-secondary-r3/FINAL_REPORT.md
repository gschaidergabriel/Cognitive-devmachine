## 1. Executive result

H3 established epoch-pinned topology and H4 established typed semantic firebreaks inside one Clay language. The newer mesh exposes a further ambiguity: the project has been using the word `topology` for several different mechanisms.

A persistent higher-order relation, a currently active spreading path, a dependency that forces recomputation, a query/candidate relation, a workspace bridge, a process/shared-memory link and an authority path are **not the same edge**.

H5 therefore proposes:

# HYP-RC5 — Context-Gated Multi-Topology Hyperposition

Core law:

> **one semantic relation != one propagation edge != one invalidation edge != one process link != one authority path**

and:

> **higher-order identity may remain persistent while active coupling is sparse, context-gated and operation-specific.**

The result is not `hypergraph everywhere`. External 2026 evidence includes both sides: higher-order/n-ary structure can matter, but some higher-order dynamics admit exact weighted pairwise reductions. H5 therefore treats topology as a **compiler problem**: preserve the richest semantics required, then lower each operation into the simplest representation proven equivalent.

Fresh concurrent workers sharpen this further. FRANK C8 states `rich persistent state != active cognitive slice`. UnifiedDB U2 decomposes physical placement into `transaction × data × index × compute × process × fault domain × rebuild policy`. These are strongly compatible with H5 but share RAE ancestry and do not count as independent replication.

## 3. External research synthesis

### Higher-order semantics can matter

Peer-reviewed HyperGraphRAG and HyperRAG provide AI-specific cases where preserving n-ary/hypergraph structure improves tested retrieval/answer behavior. A 2026 Nature Reviews Physics review documents qualitatively different collective dynamics under higher-order interaction.

Transfer: FRANK must not assume every group/role relation is safely reducible to entity-pair edges.

### Higher-order does not imply native hypergraph execution

A 2026 Physical Review Research paper demonstrates an exact weighted-pairwise mapping for a general class of higher-order node-update dynamics. Other work shows graph expansions can preserve or lose varying amounts of hypergraph information.

H5 rule:
- direct pairwise when semantics are intrinsically pairwise;
- weighted pairwise only with operation-specific equivalence evidence;
- typed relation-node/incidence graph where it losslessly preserves n-ary identity;
- native hyperrelation when simpler/cheaper or where no valid reduction exists;
- lossy lowering must be explicit and cannot silently support effect-critical reasoning.

### Rich representation does not justify unrestricted propagation

2025–2026 hypergraph neural/diffusion research reports oversmoothing/representation-collapse risks under deep multi-way propagation. Synapse independently uses spreading activation with lateral inhibition and temporal decay in agent memory.

Transfer: relation representation and propagation gain/depth are independent experimental variables.

### Incremental dependency topology is credible

Feldera/DBSP, Differential Dataflow and Salsa provide independent implementation precedent for dependency-tracked incremental recomputation. Salsa's untracked-dependency behavior is especially compatible with H5's `UNKNOWN` state: incomplete closure should trigger conservative recomputation rather than unsafe cache reuse.

### Physical topology is separate

Apache Arrow's C Data Interface supports same-process zero-copy but explicitly does not define cross-process/persistence semantics. Shared-memory IPC literature reports potential gains but also synchronization/cache/concurrency costs. Physical topology remains workload/fault-domain/platform dependent.

## 4. Experiment 1 — n-ary identity versus naive pair flattening

`EXP-HYP-H5-NARY-INTEGRITY-001`

Local synthetic corpus:
- 3,000 entities;
- four-role relation instances;
- query first three roles, retrieve fourth;
- 10,000 sampled queries per density;
- exact relation-instance identity versus naive entity-pair co-occurrence intersection.

| relation instances | pairwise recall | mean false candidates | mean precision |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5,000 | 0.9990 | 0.0001 | 0.99895 |
| 10,000 | 0.9987 | 0.0100 | 0.99384 |
| 30,000 | 0.9989 | 0.1915 | 0.91132 |
| 60,000 | 0.9994 | 1.4151 | 0.54108 |
| 100,000 | 0.9988 | 5.9096 | 0.17179 |

Naive pair flattening kept the true relation reachable but created rapidly increasing false completions as relation density rose.

The correct conclusion is **relation-instance identity must survive**. It is not proof that the physical container must be a native hypergraph; a typed relation-node/incidence lowering can be lossless.

## 5. Experiment 2 — representation versus propagation

`EXP-HYP-H5-PROPAGATION-002`

Local activation-diffusion proxy:
- 3,000 nodes;
- 10 communities;
- 9,000 hyperedges;
- mostly within-community, some cross-community relations;
- ten seeds, ten propagation steps.

At step 10:

| policy | target-context mass | normalized community entropy | active nodes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| UNBOUNDED | 0.29557 | 0.93182 | 3,000 |
| HARD gate | 1.00000 | ~0 | 300 |
| current ADAPTIVE | 0.99886 | 0.00484 | 348 |

Unbounded propagation reaches the whole graph and loses context specificity. Hard gating preserves context but removes nearly all cross-context diversity. The current adaptive construction also remains too close to hard gating.

Therefore H5 **does not** claim the diversity-reserve problem is solved. It establishes only that richer higher-order representation does not require unrestricted propagation.

This is an activation/noise proxy, not a direct HGNN oversmoothing or cognitive-utility measurement.

## 6. Experiment 3 — contextual invalidation

`EXP-HYP-H5-CONTEXT-INVALIDATION-003`

300,000 synthetic operations:
- 24 domains;
- six alternative contexts;
- three dependencies/context;
- ~8% probability that one true active dependency is hidden from the observed closure.

Results:
- true active-context invalidation: **23.519%**;
- static union reject: **75.729%**;
- static-union false rejects: **52.210% of all operations**;
- narrow observed reject: **22.897%**;
- narrow unsafe accepts: **1,864 / 300,000 = 0.6213%**;
- `UNKNOWN` + broaden reject: **27.717%**;
- `UNKNOWN` + broaden unsafe accepts: **0**;
- conservative false rejects: **4.198%**.

Static union is safe in this construction but massively overinvalidates. Active-context topology is much cheaper when closure is known. Hidden dependencies make narrow scope unsafe. Explicit `UNKNOWN` plus broadening removed all constructed unsafe accepts while retaining most of the efficiency gain.

This gives contextual Hyperposition a concrete coexistence mechanism: inactive alternatives stay represented without forcing every operation to recompute.

## 7. Experiment 4 — physical topology / IPC

`EXP-HYP-H5-IPC-004`

The first attempted execution failed technically and is discarded.

The successful local CPython multiprocessing rerun compares:
- copying payload through `multiprocessing.Pipe`;
- copying payload into preallocated `SharedMemory` plus a tiny readiness signal.

| payload | Pipe p50 | SharedMemory+copy+signal p50 | Pipe/SHM |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 KiB | 2.253 µs | 1.763 µs | 1.278x |
| 64 KiB | 7.666 µs | 3.135 µs | 2.445x |
| 1 MiB | 146.743 µs | 31.286 µs | 4.690x |

In this local proxy SharedMemory wins every tested p50 and its advantage grows with payload.

This does **not** establish universal shared-memory superiority. The test omits realistic contention, cache visibility, multi-reader coordination, lifecycle, crash recovery, Rust/Android implementation and security/fault-domain cost. It only confirms that payload movement is a material independent physical-topology variable.

## 8. HYP-RC5 — six topologies over one Clay world

### T1 Semantic relation topology
Persistent exact identity:
- relation instance and roles;
- semantic class;
- provenance;
- valid/transaction time;
- collection algebra;
- conflict/revocation state.

### T2 Active propagation topology
Operation/frame-specific:
- active relation subset;
- gain/depth;
- decay;
- inhibition;
- activation budget;
- diversity reserve;
- graph/activation epochs.

A semantic relation need not propagate now.

### T3 Dependency/invalidation topology
Only dependencies that can change the current operation force recomputation when closure is known.

`KNOWN_VALID | KNOWN_INVALID | UNKNOWN`

UNKNOWN broadens, instruments, recomputes, escalates or denies/defers consequential effects.

### T4 Selection/workspace topology
Binds:
- query plan;
- TCQS collection semantics;
- candidate set/ranking;
- system WorkspaceEpisode;
- reader projection;
- model-internal uptake receipt.

### T5 Physical topology
U2 sharpens this into a placement vector:

`transaction domain × data placement × index placement × compute placement × process domain × fault domain × rebuild policy`.

Possible routes include same-process handles, shared memory, IPC, specialized stores and services. Cost optimization is constrained by correctness/replay/security, not vice versa.

### T6 Authority topology
Capability/effect authority remains externally mediated. Propagation, selection, GWT, model uptake or colocation never mint authority.

## 9. Contextual alternatives without destructive collapse

H5's answer to the original Hyperposition question is:

1. retain alternatives canonically with explicit context predicates/facets, roles, provenance and time;
2. compile a small context-active semantic/dependency/propagation slice per operation;
3. keep unresolved conflicts/unknowns unresolved rather than overwriting them;
4. use static union/global validation only as conservative fallback under UNKNOWN closure;
5. receipt the active topology so replay and causal experiments know what actually participated.

This is contextual/set-valued software state. `Entanglement` remains an engineering analogy, not a quantum claim.

## 10. Anti-attractor consequence

Hypofusion H1 and FRANK C8 expose a key failure mode: immutable truth does not prevent a utility-trained salience field from repeatedly surfacing the same authentic-but-misleading association and becoming a self-confirming attractor.

H5 therefore treats gain/depth/inhibition/diversity as topology controls, not merely model parameters.

Required shadows:
- bounded gain;
- lateral inhibition;
- decay;
- novelty/diversity reserve;
- role/temporal-shuffled fields;
- adversarial utility/reward poisoning;
- exact immutable anchors.

A firebreak can prove authenticity. It cannot prove task relevance.

## 11. Nested workspace consequence

GWT R5 remains intact:

`system selection != model-internal uptake`.

Both are topology levels downstream of candidate formation. A correct system winner may fail to become privileged internal state; internal model dynamics may arise without system selection. Neither is effect authority.

This demands independent intervention rather than a bundled `GWT ON/OFF` comparison.
